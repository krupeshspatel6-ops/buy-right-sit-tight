**What is it?**

Gimat is a Turkish retailer people call "Turkey's Costco in the making." The founder studied Costco closely and is trying to copy what makes it work. Today it's tiny — just two stores — and the whole idea is what happens if it grows into many.

**Why now?**

I came across a clear write-up making the case for Gimat, and the more I sat with it, the more it made sense. This is my first stock outside the US (bought in foreign currency) — a Turkish company, bought in lira — and I put real money in, not a tiny starter.

**The bet**

A small, well-run retailer that keeps expanding can compound for a long time. What I like is that the downside feels protected — Gimat owns its real estate and already has stores that work — while the upside comes from opening more. If they can add about one store a year, the thesis I read says it could be worth roughly 10x over ten years, dividends reinvested. I don't know if that happens, but I like a bet where I understand what protects me on the way down.

**What could go wrong?**

Plenty. Retail is a brutally hard business, and growing a store footprint is exactly where a lot of retailers break. And this is Turkey — the lira has been weak, so even if the business does great in lira, a falling exchange rate can eat my return in dollars. It's a market I don't know as well as the US.

**I plan to add a lot more to this position.** This is just my initial buy. As I follow the story and my conviction grows, I fully intend to put much more money into Gimat over time. Every add will show up here as a dated entry in this same chapter — never a rewrite of what I wrote today.
