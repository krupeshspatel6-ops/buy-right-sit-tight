I read Thomas Phelps' *100 to 1 in the Stock Market* and one line stuck:
**"Buy right and sit tight."** Paul Samuelson said the other half:
investing should be like **watching paint dry** — if you want excitement,
take $800 to Las Vegas.

Everyone quotes these lines. Almost nobody is willing to be watched
actually living them. So this book is me, in the chair, in public.

The rules are simple:

1. **Every stock I buy with my own money opens a new chapter** — published
   within 24 hours of the fill, with the real price and a timestamp.
2. **A chapter only closes when I sell.** If I never sell, the chapter
   never ends. That's the point.
3. **Nothing is edited after publication.** Corrections get appended,
   dated, below the original. My bad calls stay on the page next to my
   good ones.
4. **Every chapter states, on day one, what would make me sell** — so
   future-me can't quietly rewrite the story.

The scoreboard on the front page compares every dollar in this book with
the same dollars put into the S&P 500 on the same days. If, after five
years, this book beats the index — it will have been worth writing. If it
doesn't, it will at least be an honest record of a man watching paint dry.

This is a personal journal, not investment advice. The title belongs to
Phelps, the tagline to Samuelson. The sitting is mine.
